# What “rotation” means — five different claims, not one observation

The research question is whether an **invariant, physically identifiable universal rotation** exists. A common geometric vocabulary does not mean a common observed physical mechanism.

## 1. Rotation of spatial vectors and bodies

An active Euclidean 3-D rotation is R∈SO(3): RᵀR=I and det R=+1. It can move a vector without any physical object rotating. A rotating rigid velocity field around z has u=(-ωy,ωx,0), with curl u=(0,0,2ω). A coordinate-axis choice and a material angular velocity require physical identification.

**Null:** u=(c,0,0) or u=∇φ for smooth φ has zero curl.

## 2. Rotation of a complex scalar *phase*

For ψ=ρ exp(iθ) with ρ≥0, the complex argument is a point on an internal U(1) circle. An integer winding of θ around a loop can be topologically protected when ψ stays nonzero on the loop.

```text
n = (1/2π) ∮ dθ ∈ Z
```

A **real-valued scalar** has no U(1) angle by itself. A time-oscillating real scalar and a phase-winding complex scalar are different claims.

**Null:** φ(x,t)=constant. **Caveat:** θ is undefined at ψ=0; in a local gauge theory the gauge-invariant circulation uses phase and gauge connection together.

## 3. Rotation represented by spinors / Clifford rotors

An element of the spin group (e.g. Spin(3)≅SU(2)) represents SO(3) action via a double cover. Two spin elements ±R induce the same action on ordinary 3-vectors. Interferometric spin-1/2 behavior is experimentally real. This is **not** an observable angular speed common to all fields.

**Null:** the identity rotor describes no rotation.

## 4. Rotation of electromagnetic polarization

A change in polarization direction along a ray may be due to Faraday propagation in magnetized plasma, instrumentation, cosmic birefringence, or other mechanisms. Its angle is a **polarization-space rotation**, not automatically the vorticity of a spacetime congruence. These possibilities have distinct frequency and parity signatures.

## 5. Rotation of a cosmological observer congruence

In GR, choose a timelike unit field uᵃ for a physical congruence of observers. Its covariant gradient decomposes into expansion, shear, acceleration and antisymmetric vorticity. Schematically:

```text
∇_b u_a = spatial_expansion + spatial_shear + spatial_vorticity
          + acceleration/time terms
ω_ab = h_a^c h_b^d ∇_[d u_c]
h_ab = g_ab + u_a u_b  (signature −,+,+,+)
```

The value is tied to the **specified physical congruence** and is not equivalent to a coordinate grid appearing to rotate. Nonzero cosmic vorticity has observable implications that depend on the metric, shear, matter, and null geodesics. Planck's Bianchi VIIh constraints test a specified family, not every conceivable rotational model.

## What a single universal theory would have to supply

- One covariant physical generator / order parameter with explicitly stated units and gauge/observer dependence.
- An action or field equations with a valid stress-energy tensor, conservation constraints, and stable dynamics.
- Derivations linking each sector, not just similar algebraic notation.
- At least one **quantitatively new** prediction distinguishable from ordinary local vortices, known GR, plasma propagation, and standard scalar phase physics.
- A model-specific null, independent data custody and predefined stopping rule.

**Do not convert** “rotation occurs in many different theories” into “all fields share a nonzero cosmic angular velocity.” The latter is a new, presently unsupported physical hypothesis.
